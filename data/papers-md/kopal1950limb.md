Thus far the coefficients u ; in equation (2) were left’ unrestricted - save for the obvious re-
quirement that J(y) is to remain positive over the whole visible hemisphere, and that dJ/dy remains
negative. If these coefficients were really arbitrary, the problem of predicting theoretical light or

A glance at the results discloses that, on the whole, the photometric effects of rotation of distorted
stars are not very sensitive to the behavior of J(y) in the neighborhood of y= 90°. The deviations
of the actual distribution of brightness near the limb from a simple cosine law are found to magnify the
effects of the second and third harmonic distortion by one and four per cent, respectively, and to dimin-
ish the coefficient of the fourth harmonic by about two per cent. The differences between a quadratic
and a quartic approximation to the actual law of darkening, such as represented by equations (21.0) and
(21.1) prove to have altogether negligible consequences. This all leads us to conclude that, for the
time being and probably for several years to come, an interpretation of even the most precisely observed
light curves should not call for the retention of more than the quadratic term in equation (2); while,
in ordinary cases, a failure to consider this quadratic term should produce barely sensible effects.
